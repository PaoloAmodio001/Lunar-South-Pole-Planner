# Project Summary — Lunar South Pole Planner

Lunar South Pole Planner is an open, locally run Python and web application that helps people see when sunlight and Earth line-of-sight are available at candidate lunar south pole sites. It queries the NASA/JPL Horizons API for Sun and Earth positions seen from three candidate sites (Shackleton rim, Connecting Ridge and Nobile rim 1) and combines them with NASA Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter terrain models: 5 m/pixel LOLA elevation maps from NASA Goddard's Planetary Geodesy Data Archive and a PDS-archived LROC NAC stereo terrain model of the Shackleton rim. The tool computes terrain horizon profiles, slope and roughness, Sun and Earth visibility windows, a first-order radio link budget, preliminary rover routes and energy estimates. It presents these through a public 3D Moon globe, a free Educators section and a login-protected planner dashboard. An offline SPICE ephemeris agrees with Horizons to within 1.4 × 10⁻⁵ degrees, and two independent NASA terrain products give a consistent Sun visibility. All outputs are preliminary diagnostics for research and education. They are not flight-certified.
