SOLAR ILLUMINATION ANALYSIS

Solar illumination: the Sun is up, the terrain decides

How it works

Horizon profile from the DEM (observer 2 m, 2° azimuth steps, curved-Moon geometry)

Sun position from JPL Horizons or offline Skyfield + NAIF SPICE (DE421)

Grid-to-north azimuth offset derived from the raster CRS

What the evidence shows

LOLA and LROC give the same Sun-visible fraction to a median 99.9% (worst 95.5%)

Not yet compared with published illumination maps or in-situ data

Terrain-aware windows run from the Mission Engine CLI; the Web UI shows geometric windows

IMPACT AND FUTURE DEVELOPMENT

Scientific relevance, impact and next steps

Why it matters

Sunlight and Earth line-of-sight drive power, thermal and communications design near the pole

Makes NASA ephemerides and LRO terrain usable without GIS or SPICE expertise

Free Educators section with labs on live Horizons data

Known limits

Preliminary diagnostics: not flight-certified, not operationally validated

Illumination not yet checked against published maps or in-situ data

Energy and thermal models are first-order; three sites only

Next steps

Terrain-aware Sun/Earth windows inside the Web UI

Compare with published LOLA illumination studies (Mazarico 2011; Barker 2021)

More PGDA sites, HTTPS deployment, browser end-to-end tests

Open data in, transparent diagnostics out: every number on these slides can be traced to a file in the project.
